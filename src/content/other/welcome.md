## The case in two paragraphs

In December 1948, a man's body was found propped against a seawall at Somerton Park beach in Adelaide. He had no identification. The labels on his clothing had been removed. In a hidden pocket of his trousers, sewn shut, was a torn scrap of paper bearing the words "Tamám Shud" — Persian for "ended" or "finished" — torn from a rare edition of Edward FitzGerald's translation of *The Rubáiyát of Omar Khayyám*.

The book itself turned up in a car parked nearby. Inside the back cover, written in faint pencil, was a sequence of letters that nobody has convincingly decoded in seventy-five years. Or so the story goes.
