The evidence, layer by layer

One established fact sits at the bottom; everything above it is held at a stated confidence. The cards below separate what is documented from what is finding, caveat, or rival reading.

LAYER 1 · ESTABLISHED

The Somerton Man is Carl Webb

2022 DNA identification via rootless-hair genome data and a 4,000-person family tree (Abbott & Fitzpatrick). Formal coronial confirmation outstanding.

LAYER 2 · INFERENCE

Webb was searching for a woman

The documented thread points to Glenelg: her number on the book, his death 400 m from her door. The rival Dorothy-in-Bute claim has no 1948 record behind it and a 1949 Melbourne electoral roll against it.

LAYER 3 · SPECULATION

A paternity belief drove the search

DNA excludes Webb as Robin Thomson's father. The motive survives only as something Webb may have believed — never as fact.

LAYER 4 · PENDING FALSIFICATION

The code is a travel record

Consistent with the evidence, modestly supported by frequency analysis, untested by sequence-and-connectivity. The Map Lab shows both the support and the warning.

Documented

The body and the scene

Found 6:30 am, 1 December 1948, against the seawall at Somerton, opposite the Crippled Children's Home, legs extended, half-smoked cigarette on the collar. Seen motionless the previous evening.

Autopsy: acute gastric haemorrhage, spleen about three times normal size, last meal a pasty 3–4 hours before death. Poison suspected; none identified.

Documented

The pockets

An unused second-class rail ticket for the 10:50 am to Henley Beach. A used bus ticket toward Glenelg from North Terrace. Juicy Fruit, an American comb, Army Club packet holding Kensitas cigarettes. No wallet, no papers; clothing labels removed.

Documented

The suitcase

Checked at Adelaide Railway Station 11:00–11:15 am, 30 November; recovered 14 January 1949. Tied to the body by orange waxed thread matching a trouser repair. Garments marked T. Keane — the name of Webb's brother-in-law.

Documented

The slip and the book

"Tamám Shud" — ended — rolled in a fob pocket, torn from a Rubáiyát that surfaced from a car parked near Glenelg. On the inside back cover: the pencilled letters, a telephone number, and by some accounts a second number never identified.

Documented

The number and the address

X3239 reached Jessica Thomson at 90A Moseley Street, Glenelg — about 400 m from the body. The number was semi-public: Prosper Thomson's car classifieds printed it from July 1947, the one paper-trailed route by which Webb could have held it.

52 Moseley Street, given in some secondary sources, is wrong; 90A is the 1948 electoral-roll address.

Caveat

The code is a copy of a copy

Faint pencil indentations, photographed and reportedly over-written. First letters of lines 1, 3 and 4 are disputed between W and M. Any reading that hangs on a single letter hangs on a seventy-five-year-old rubbing.

WRGOABABD MLIAOI (struck out) WTBIMPANETP MLIABOAIAQC ITTMTSAMSTGAB
Finding

Initials, not cipher

1949 naval cryptographers: letter frequencies sit closer to the initial letters of English words than to any cipher table. University of Adelaide work later eliminated 20+ cipher types and reached the same conclusion. The question becomes: initials of what?

Caveat

The test that cannot fail

All 17 distinct letters have at least one Adelaide-reachable candidate. With 575 verified stations plus tram and bus stops, set-membership matching always succeeds — for the code, or for any random string. RPFA exists to replace that broken test with a constrained one.

Rival

Horse-betting initials

Webb bet on horses; Abbott favours a betting-list reading; and it benefits from the same initialism finding. Co-equal with the itinerary reading on current evidence. The itinerary's edge: the coastal G–A–B ending, and a searcher's obvious reason to keep a record.

Rival

Espionage and suicide

The spy reading is badly weakened by the Webb identification: a Melbourne instrument maker with a failed marriage and no intelligence trace. The suicide reading is strong and compatible — the letters concern how he spent his last days, not how they ended.

What this shows — and what it cannot

At small radii almost nowhere qualifies; full coverage opens up only in a narrow inner north-western band, with Q the binding constraint. That bounds where a compact search base could sit. It does not prove the letters are places: that requires the sequence-and-connectivity test, which this lens deliberately does not perform.
